Spark. Living Day

Privacy Policy for Spark. Living Day

Last updated: November 20, 2026

Spark. Living Day is made by Aaron Paul Laird (Codex of Reality, codexofreality.org). This policy is short because the app is built to have almost nothing to say: it keeps your record on your phone, and it cannot send it anywhere.

What the app does with your information

The app stores its record — your Spark, your garden, your kept days, your keepsakes, your settings — only on your device, in the app's own storage. The app holds no internet permission, so it has no way to transmit anything. There is no account, no server, and no analytics.

The two senses, and their permissions

The app asks for two permissions, each only when you choose to open that sense, and each refused just as easily:

  • Calendar (READ_CALENDAR): if you grant it, the app reads your calendar events on the device so it can set them beside your days on the Pattern calendar. Events are read and shown on the device only.
  • Photos (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES; READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE on older Android): if you grant it, the app reads your photos' dates and small thumbnails on the device so it can set your pictures by day and propose groupings. It does not copy, upload, or alter your photos.

Deny either permission and the app works without that sense. You can revoke either at any time in Android's settings.

Sharing, by your hand only

The app's record can be saved to a file or sent through Android's share sheet — only when you tap to do so, only to where you send it. That file is yours; whoever you give it to can read it. Nothing is shared automatically, ever.

Children

The app is a general-audience calendar and companion. It collects no information from anyone, including children, because it collects no information at all.

Deleting your information

Everything the app keeps is on your device. Letting go of your Spark inside the app clears its record; uninstalling the app removes everything it stored. There is no copy anywhere else to delete.

Changes to this policy

If a future version changes any of this — above all, if it ever asks for a permission this version does not — this page will be updated and the new version's listing will say so.
