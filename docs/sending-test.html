The Sending Test

Experimental

People talk about telepathy — one mind reaching another — and about foresight, knowing something before it happens. Talk is cheap either way. This tool does not ask you to believe or to disbelieve. It lets you run the test cleanly: a fixed number of tries, targets sealed before the first guess, and a score measured honestly against chance.

The protocol — read this first

How the test stays honest

  1. You fix the length first. A session is 20 or 40 tries, chosen before anything is drawn. You cannot quietly stop early on a lucky streak or stretch a session until it looks good.
  2. The targets are sealed. When the session starts, the page draws the whole list of targets from a secret seed — a starting number the draw grows from — and locks it in a box. The box's fingerprint is shown to you before the first guess: a long code made from the list (a SHA-256 hash). If the list were changed afterward, the fingerprint would no longer match, and at the end you can check it yourself.
  3. Chance is one in four. Each try offers four symbols and exactly one is the target. Guessing blindly scores about 5 hits in 20 tries, or 10 in 40 — and luck alone sometimes runs higher. The scoring below says exactly how often.
  4. Everything is revealed at the end. The seed and the full target list are shown, the seal is re-checked in front of you, and the session is kept on this device only. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

      Reading a result

      What would count as evidence

      Three things, all built into this tool: targets sealed before the first guess, a trial count fixed in advance, and a record anyone can re-check. A claim tested any other way — targets chosen after, sessions stopped when they look good, records nobody can verify — has not really been tested at all.

      And the hard part: even a score outside the luck line, once, is not proof. Luck deals somebody an unlikely session every day. What would count is the same result, outside the line, repeating across many sealed sessions.

      Why small samples mislead

      Luck runs in streaks

      In 20 tries, luck alone reaches 8 hits about 1 time in 10 — that feels like something, and it is nothing but luck doing what luck does. It reaches 9 or more about 1 time in 25. Short runs swing wildly; that is why this tool fixes the count up front and why its verdict always names how often luck alone would do at least as well.

      History — labeled as history

      What others tried

      In the 1970s through the 1990s, researchers ran "Ganzfeld" sessions: a receiver resting in soft, even light and sound, a sender viewing a picture or a clip, then the receiver picking it from four choices — the same one-in-four shape as this tool. Some combined reports put the hit rate above chance; other researchers argued the methods leaked or the counting was generous. Decades later it is still argued, both ways. The lesson that survives the argument is the protocol: seal the targets, fix the count, keep the record — which is what this page does.
