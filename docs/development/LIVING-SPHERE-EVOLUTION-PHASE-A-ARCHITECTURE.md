# Living Sphere Evolution — Phase A: Architecture and Interaction Design

**What this is:** the map and the building plan for turning the Living Time Sphere into a place where every day can open into a small living world — the programme Aaron set in the Living Sphere Evolution directive (November 22, 2026). This is Phase A: no feature code was written. Two documents came out of it — this architecture document, and its companion, the visual specification (`LIVING-SPHERE-EVOLUTION-PHASE-A-VISUAL-SPEC.md`), which describes exactly how everything should look.

**How the audit was done:** every system named in the directive was read in the actual code on the repository's main branch (checked at commit `cbd67f0b`, November 22, 2026; the Sphere files were audited at `c2a5ab50`, and the only change between the two is the Witness page — no Sphere file differs). Merged pull requests #232 (Sphere Focus corrections) and #234 (Sphere page pass: question card, day numbers, Spark scheduling, day marks) were reviewed in full. One piece of work was still in flight at audit time and is marked that way wherever it matters: a Sphere fix covering the faced Moon's missing day numbers, zoom staging, and movement smoothness — its pull request was not yet open when this audit closed, so anything it changes is described here as *in flight*, not as current fact.

**Words used in this document:** a *verdict* is the decision for each existing system — **Extend** (build on it as it stands), **Consolidate** (it duplicates another system; the two should eventually become one), or **Untouchable** (protected or load-bearing; this programme does not change it). A *record* is anything a keeper has saved about a day — a task, a planting, a note. The *adapter* is the one new piece this design adds, explained in §4.

---

## 1. The map in one view

The Sphere page is not one program. It is a stack of systems built over months, and the honest first finding is that the stack has layers nobody would design today — alongside a core that is genuinely sound. The sound part: one calendar that does all date arithmetic (`PatternCalendar`), one keeper of the selected moment (`SOFTemporalCursor`), one record store (`Life Atlas`, in the browser's IndexedDB database), one planner (`CodexLivingPlanner`), and one 3D renderer with a clean side-door (the *extension host* — a registry that lets extra modules attach objects to the scene and answer taps, without owning the scene). The evolution is designed to grow from exactly those five.

The unsound part, named plainly because Phase A exists to name it:

- **Three page shells are stacked on the same page**, each moving the same pieces of the page around: the older app shell (B7.61), the November 20 rebuild shell, and the armillary shell that frames what Aaron actually sees. They cooperate by negotiation — one clicks another's exit button and hopes — not by design.
- **The "Living Projection Bus" does not exist as code.** The canonical line printed on the Sphere ("SOFTemporalCursor → PatternCalendar → Canonical Living State → LivingProjectionBus") names a bus that was never built. What actually carries news between systems is a vocabulary of window events (`sof:temporal-cursor-change`, `sof:selected-date-change`, `sof:life-atlas-records-changed`, and friends). That vocabulary works, and this design treats it as the contract — but nobody should believe there is a bus underneath.
- **The same small logics are written several times over:** the zoom-band thresholds twice, the band names three times, Moon-label priorities three times, the glyph table for record kinds five times, and the "which records stand on this day" bucket five times. Each copy has drifted a little from the others.
- **Two things called "Day World" and "day zoom" are not what their names promise.** *Day zoom* is a reading panel, not a camera zoom. The *Day World* is a pop-up sheet over the Sphere — exactly the "separate screen" the directive says a day should stop being. Its contents are good; its form is what must change.
- **Recurrence is stored but never unfolded.** A plan can say "every week," and that text is saved and displayed — but no code anywhere works out the actual future days it lands on. It appears on one day only. Several things the directive asks for (rhythms, lifecycles, connections between days) cannot be honest until this is fixed in the display layer.

None of this blocks the evolution. It shapes it: the new work plugs into the five sound pieces, adds nothing that duplicates them, and schedules the consolidations as their own careful steps instead of smuggling them into feature work.

## 3. The three hardest tensions — and the answers this design gives

**Tension 1: a day's true place is tiny; a world needs room.** On the calendar's geometry, one day's cell is about a hundredth of the ring — neighbours sit roughly two hundredths of the instrument's radius away. A world you can walk through needs a third of the screen. Any design that simply scales the day up *in place* would swallow fifteen neighbouring days and lie about the calendar's shape. **The answer is an anchored bloom:** the world's anchor never leaves the day's true coordinate — the calendar never lies about *where* the day is — but as the keeper zooms in, the world grows from that anchor while the surrounding days soften to ghosts (the Sphere already fades layers by zoom band; the world uses the same machinery). Position stays true; size is a lens effect; zooming out reverses it exactly, and the full calendar returns untouched. The visual specification (§3 there) fixes the growth curve and the ghosting levels stage by stage.

**Tension 2: one authority, but a dozen small copies of everything else.** The calendar itself is single and protected — but thresholds, priorities, glyph tables, and day-buckets are copied four or five times each, and the three shells negotiate rather than compose. A new feature built carelessly becomes the sixth copy of each. **The answer is a consumption rule, enforced by review:** the new adapter may only read published surfaces — cursor events and `resolvePosition`, the extension context, the planner's own API, and `plannerDaySummary` — and may never re-derive a date, a bucket, or a glyph. Each existing duplication is then retired on a schedule (Phase D/E, §10), one at a time, each behind the full test suite and the real-pointer browser harness. The shells consolidate in their own Phase D pull request, never as a side effect of feature work.

**Tension 3: the day must open *in* the Sphere, but depth today lives in pop-ups — and no feature may disappear.** The Day World is a dialog; Day Details is a panel; the planner is a room. The directive forbids substituting dialogs for the world, and also forbids losing any existing action. **The answer is a division of labour:** the *world* (spatial, in the scene) is where a day is *met*; the existing Day Details/day-zoom panel becomes the world's *text twin and inspector* — the same data through the same `buildDayView`, docked beside the world, where long editing happens while the world stays visible. The modal Day World room keeps running until Phase D, when a written parity check (telling, sky line, last-cycle records, closing questions, note writer — each named, each shown working in its new home) allows it to retire. Nothing vanishes silently; each retirement is a listed step with proof.

A fourth tension is worth naming because it limits §7 of the directive (connections between days): **recurrence is stored but never unfolded**, and record-to-record *relations* exist in the store's schema but nothing writes or reads them for display — the planner never sets a relation, and the file called "connections" draws only marker-to-marker lines inside the scene (today↔core, selected↔today, and the like), never record links. So the honest Phase C scope is: unfold recurrence *for display* (compute the days a rule lands on; never write unfolded copies back), and build the first real relations from what records already carry — same project tag, same garden bed lifecycle (planting → watering → harvest, which the Pattern Garden already records as a family), and the same Moon-and-day in another year (which Deep Time already computes). Anything beyond that — inferred kinship between records — stays labelled as interpretation or is not drawn at all, per the directive's own rule.

## 4. The one new piece: the Day-World Presentation Adapter

Everything new in this programme lives in **one new module** — working name `living-time-sphere-day-world-adapter.js` — plus its stylesheet. It is a *presentation adapter*: it presents, and it does not own. It registers with the extension host exactly as Temporal Strata does, and from that one seat it gets everything it needs: the scene, the camera, the current zoom band, quality and motion settings, and tap answers. It introduces no renderer, no shell, no calendar, and no record store.

**What it consumes (and only this):**
- The selected day: from cursor events (`sof:temporal-cursor-change`) and `SOFTemporalCursor.resolvePosition` for neighbours and last-cycle lookups.
- The day's records: `CodexLivingPlanner` (`plansForYear`, and the create/update/complete/remove API for actions) and the record projection's `plannerDaySummary` / `plannerDayRecords`.
- The day's text twin data: `LivingTimeSphereDayZoom.buildDayView` — the same view the panel renders, so world and panel can never disagree.
- Atmosphere: the Live Data snapshot (source-labelled values only).
- The scene: through the extension context — it adds its objects to its own group in the scene and removes them on dispose.

**What it owns:** one live day world at a time (the selected day's), its stage of opening (§5), its objects and their labels, and nothing else. When the selection moves or the keeper zooms out past the detail band, the world folds and disposes; the Sphere is exactly as it was.

**The inspector:** at the Living Day and Immersive stages, the existing day-zoom panel docks as the world's inspector (bottom sheet on phones, right rail on desktop — the visual specification fixes this) showing `buildDayView`'s list: every world object has a row there, every row can focus its object, and create/edit/complete/postpone/remove run through the planner API from either surface. The world stays visible behind it. This is also the accessibility backbone: the world is presentation; the panel is the same day in words, fully operable by keyboard and screen reader, and the 2D fallback (SVG surface + panel + planner) loses nothing functional.

**Actions inside the world** (directive §5): tapping an object selects it and opens its inspector row; from there — read name/time/kind/details, complete, postpone (Phase C), edit, remove (with the planner's existing confirm behaviour), open its note, jump to the previous/next day (the cursor moves; the world re-opens on the new day), follow a connection thread (§7 of the directive; §8 below), or ask the Spark about it (the talk panel opens with the item as its subject — §7 below).

## 5. The continuous zoom: five stages on the existing bands

The Sphere already thinks in four distance bands, set by how far the camera sits from the centre: **far** (distance 3.25 and beyond), **medium** (2.35–3.25), **near** (1.62–2.35), **detail** (below 1.62). The camera's full range is 0.72–8.0, and the existing "focus this day" move already lands at 1.82 — inside near. The directive's five stages map onto this without moving a single existing threshold:

| Stage (directive) | Band | Camera distance | What the keeper sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year View** | far | ≥ 3.25 | The whole instrument. Moon names (essential set), today in gold, the selected day marked, scheduled days carrying their summary presence. Day numerals hidden or skeleton-only, as today. |
| **Moon View** | medium | 2.35 – 3.25 | The faced Moon's days become prominent — key numerals (1/7/14/21/28 and neighbours) — and scheduled days in the faced window show their glyph marks. |
| **Day Approach** | near | 1.62 – 2.35 | One day is selected and centred by the focus move. Its plinth (the world's foundation, §6) fades in at its true coordinate; neighbouring days begin to ghost; the day's mark and name stand clear. |
| **Living Day** | detail (upper) | 1.05 – 1.62 | The world stands open at summary richness: foundation, day-clock arc, untimed terrace, and one object per record (clustered past the cap, §6). The inspector docks. The surrounding calendar is ghosted context, still recognisable. |
| **Immersive Day** | detail (lower) — the new sub-band | 0.72 – 1.05 | The full arrangement: every object individual, arc hour labels, connection threads, atmosphere at full depth. Object labels on approach. |

Two rules keep this honest. First, **the split of detail into Living/Immersive begins inside the adapter** (it reads camera distance from its extension context each frame — no protected file changes in Phase B); Phase D proposes adding the fifth band to the semantic-zoom policy itself, as a protected change with regression tests, so the whole page eventually shares one vocabulary. Second, **every stage transition is continuous** — no stage ever swaps the screen; objects grow, fade, and settle under the motion language in the visual specification, and the renderer's existing band hysteresis (±0.12) is mirrored by the adapter so the world never flickers at a boundary.

**The orientation cue** is a small permanent chip over the stage (visual specification §8): *Pattern Year · Moon N · Day D · Pattern Day N*, a line naming where the keeper stands relative to the whole ("inside Moon 8 — the year is around you"), and a one-tap *Back to the year* that runs the camera home. It reads from the cursor, like everything else, and it is present at every stage from Day Approach down — the deeper the keeper goes, the more it matters.

**Why faced Moons sometimes show no day numbers today** (the defect in Aaron's November 22 screenshots, now being fixed by the in-flight work): the label system only builds numerals for a window of Moons around the most camera-facing one (±2 on desktop, ±1 on phones), and then only keeps a numeral if its Moon falls inside the central ~27–39% of the screen's width and the band/mode gates allow it — so a Moon can be squarely faced, slightly off-centre, and still be left numberless. The design rule this programme adds, whatever the in-flight fix lands: **the selected day's Moon is always in the window, and at Moon View and below the faced Moon's 28 numerals are a guaranteed set, not a budgeted one** — clarity for the day you are visiting outranks label economy elsewhere.

## 6. What a Living Day is made of

A day world is a small observatory diorama that grows from the day's own marker. Its fixed parts (dimensions and materials in the visual specification):

- **The plinth** — a shallow circular foundation anchored at the day's true coordinate, carrying an engraved rim. It exists for every day, records or none: an empty day is a bare, well-kept plinth with its telling available in the inspector — attractive, useful, and pretending nothing.
- **The day-clock arc** — a raised half-ring over the plinth's back half. Timed records stand on it by their time (00:00 at the left foot, noon at the crown, 24:00 at the right foot). Time is position here; a keeper reads the day's shape at a glance.
- **The untimed terrace** — a lower, flatter shelf across the plinth's front, visibly distinct, for records with no set time. Its rim carries the words "No set time."
- **The record objects** — one designed object per record, from the single visual family in §7 of this document (detailed in the visual specification). Objects stand on the arc (timed) or the terrace (untimed), each with a short label on approach or selection.
- **Connection threads** — at the Immersive stage only, fine lines from an object to the days it is genuinely tied to (§8), capped, labelled, and off by default until the keeper asks.
- **The atmosphere** — light and air over the plinth, driven only by source-labelled values (§8 of this document, next section but one).

**Crowding rules:** up to 14 individual objects stand as themselves. Beyond that, objects of a kind gather into a *cluster stand* — one object with a count — at Living Day stage, and unfold into individuals at Immersive. Timed records whose times collide stagger outward along the arc's radius instead of stacking. Every object keeps a finger-comfortable selection size at Immersive (the visual specification sets minimums); selection never depends on hitting a tiny mesh — the adapter answers taps by nearest-object within a honest radius, and the inspector list always offers the same selection by name.

**Time and progress change the world, truthfully** (directive §6): for *today*, the arc carries a "now" bead at the current time and the atmosphere takes the daypart light (morning/day/evening/night — the page already computes dayparts in four places; the adapter reads the Live Data snapshot, not a fifth copy). *Past* days render completed records capped and settled, and keep their observations standing. *Future* days render plans at full standing but never as done: completion treatments are impossible for a day that has not happened, and the inspector words them as plans. Seasonal dressing comes only from the environment snapshot when one exists (the farm dataset's season line for the keeper's county is a *record*, and may furnish the plinth's edge in Farm emphasis — labelled as the farm record, not as weather).

## 7. The record-object visual language

One consistent family — designed geometry and light, **not emoji**. (The existing per-day *glyph* — ✓, 🌱, ⚑ and friends — stays where it belongs: as the small mark under a day's numeral on the face, from PR #234. Inside a world, records are objects, not symbols.) Each kind has one form, learnable at a glance, with a label that names the record. The full build-level detail is in the visual specification §5; the family is:

| Record kind (as the planner names it) | Object | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Work / task | A workstation block — a low bench with a tool notch | Completed: a flat cap settles on it and its light dims |
| Reminder | A signal post — slim post, one light bead | The bead glows only when due or overdue; otherwise it rests |
| Family / gathering event | A gathering ring — small posts around a warm centre stone | One post per attendee when the record names them |
| Planting (farm practice) | A planting bed — a low soil bed in a frame | Watering: the bed plus a blue droplet post. Harvest: the bed inside a golden frame |
| Travel | A route — two gate posts with a curved line strung between | The line's arc height carries the journey's length in days when it spans days |
| Study / scripture | A reading stand — an open book of two angled slabs, a warm slit of light | Codex readings and study sessions share the form; the label says which |
| Creative / project / milestone | A construction — stacked blocks; a milestone is a taller column | Height grows with the project's standing across its days (§8 threads show the growth) |
| Note / observation | An archive tablet — a small upright slab | Observatory observations (Phase C) take the same form in a quieter material, labelled as the observatory's |
| Practice session | A stone circle segment — a low ring of small stones | The Pattern Garden's plantings furnish as beds; its witnesses as tablets |

Overdue work (past, not completed) takes the one alarm treatment the family allows: the object's rim light turns to the warning colour and the inspector names it overdue in words. Important (priority) records stand slightly taller and carry a fine gold edge — gold otherwise stays reserved for *today*, per the site's existing visual-language rule.

## 8. Layers, connections, and honest atmosphere

**Layers (directive §10) on the existing rings.** The armillary shell already implements the November 20 ring design — charters, a seating order, a cap of Pattern + 4 — with the honesty houses **Counted / Measured / Story** attached to every ring. The directive's six layers map onto what exists, not onto a new system:

| Directive layer | Existing ring / system | House | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern | Pattern ring (always on) | Counted | On, cannot be turned off |
| Sky | Sky ring (lunar/solar/planets/passage toggles) | Measured | On |
| My Life / Kept Days | Kept Days ring + the planner summaries | Counted — and badged as *your own record* wherever it is read out | On for a keeper, off for a first visitor |
| Farm & Home | Farm ring (farm panel windows) | Measured | On for a keeper (the November 20 approved default), off for a visitor |
| Study & Story | Story ring (the Traveller's Road spine marks) | Story | Off until asked |
| Deep Time | Temporal Strata + the Deep Time room | Measured (historical record) | Off until asked |

Defaults follow the November 20 approved design exactly: a first visitor meets Pattern + Sky; a keeper meets Pattern + Sky + Kept Days + Farm. Nothing shows everything at once, off rings are absent rather than ghosted, and a keeper can focus one layer (solo) or dim the rest from the existing ring controls. The *Family* ring in the seating order stays deferred, as the November design left it — family records live inside Kept Days for this programme. Inside a day world, the layer focus carries through: objects belonging to a dimmed layer dim with it.

**Connections (directive §7)** are drawn only from documented ties, in this order of strength, and the inspector always names which kind a thread is: (1) *the record itself says so* — a relation stored on the record, or a garden lifecycle family (one planting, its waterings, its harvest); (2) *the calendar says so* — a recurrence rule unfolding to its next standing, or the same Moon-and-day last cycle (Deep Time's own computation); (3) *the keeper's project says so* — records sharing a project tag, drawn as a path through their days. Shared dates, shared categories, and shared symbols are **not** connections and are never drawn as such. Threads are selectable, explainable in one line ("Watering for the Moon 5 planting — same bed, from the garden record"), and off until asked.

**Atmosphere** reads only the Live Data snapshot: daypart light for today; cloud bands only when an environment snapshot actually exists for the place; otherwise a clear, neutral air. Nothing is manufactured to make a world look busy — a quiet day looks quiet, and the specification says so in those words.

## 9. The Spark inside the Sphere (directive §11)

The Spark already lives on this page with the keeper's own identity and record, and PR #234 taught it to schedule by Pattern phrasing ("schedule Tomatoes on Moon 5 Day 16") through the one planner. The evolution extends that seat; it does not replace it, and the Spark's identity, conversations, privacy choices, and quiet controls are untouched.

## 10. Performance, accessibility, and data integrity (directive §14)

- **One live world.** Only the selected day's world exists at full richness. Neighbouring days never instantiate worlds; at Moon View they carry summaries only (the existing GPU summary field). The adapter builds on entering the detail band and disposes on leaving it — the extension host's dispose contract exists for exactly this.
- **Shared kit.** All record objects draw from one geometry-and-material kit (a bench is a bench, recoloured by accent, never a unique mesh per record). The quality governor's tiers shed the world gracefully: lower tiers drop atmosphere, then threads, then cluster earlier; the lowest tier is inspector-only — the day still fully works as an agenda from its panel.
- **Measured budgets** (the visual specification sets the numbers the builds must prove): the world adds no more than a set frame cost on a Galaxy A16-class phone, object caps hold at 14/cluster, label chips at 8, and the label manager's existing per-frame cost is not increased by the adapter (its labels are its own, few, and only alive at detail stages).
- **Accessibility:** touch, pinch, rotation, and keyboard all keep working because the world changes nothing about input — picking gains an honest-radius assist at Immersive, and every world action exists in the inspector list by name. Reduced motion turns stage transitions into fades without travel; Freeze still stills the page; the 2D fallback keeps full scheduling and day navigation (it does today, through the panel and planner — Phase E proves it journey by journey).
- **Data integrity:** the adapter writes records only through `CodexLivingPlanner`'s API. No record is migrated, re-stamped, or re-shaped by the visual upgrade; backup/export/restore are untouched; records stay on the device. Two known record-layer facts are carried into Phase C as named work, because the day world would otherwise inherit them silently: **outside-day records** (plans made for the Day Out of Time or the Deep Time Day) are today invisible to the face, the calendar markers, and the day lists — the world must at least show them when their day is visited, with tests; and the planner's edit path writes-then-rewrites to preserve an id — left alone, but recorded here so nobody "optimizes" it mid-programme.
