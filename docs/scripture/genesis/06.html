Context: Sons of God, daughters of men, nefilim (draft, unreviewed)

Literary observations

  • Gen 6:1-4 is a short, compressed unit; its relation to the flood narrative is debated.

Background

  • The Hebrew bene ha-elohim ('sons of God') appears in other biblical passages (e.g. Job 1:6).

Where interpreters differ

  • Who are the 'sons of God'?
    • Divine/heavenly beings (attested in early Jewish interpretation, including the Septuagint-era tradition and Targum Pseudo-Jonathan (summary; unchecked))
    • Human rulers or nobles (attributed to Targum Onkelos and some medieval commentators (summary; unchecked))
    • Descendants of Seth (attributed to some early Christian writers (summary; unchecked))

Context notes for this verse (draft, unreviewed)
  • H5303 (draft-unreviewed; contested): The dataset glosses nefilim as 'giants'. The verse does not itself say 'giants': it says the nefilim were on the earth in those days, and that the 'mighty men of old' (gibborim) are linked to the passage. Proposed derivations (for example from the verb 'to fall') are proposals, not established meanings.
  • LXX Gen 6:4 — Septuagint (Greek translation tradition) (needs-source-check): The Greek translation renders nefilim with a word meaning 'giants' (gigantes). The LXX is not loaded in this edition, so this cannot be compared line by line here. LXX text not part of this build; verify against Rahlfs or another critical edition.
  • Targum Pseudo-Jonathan on Gen 6:4 — Targum (Aramaic translation tradition) (needs-source-check): Targum Pseudo-Jonathan names two fallen angels, Shemhazai and Azael, in connection with the nefilim. This is a later expansive tradition, not a lexical meaning. Not verified against an edition.
