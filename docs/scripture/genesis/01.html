Context: Creation account (draft, unreviewed)

Literary observations

  • Repeated formulae structure the days: 'God said', 'and it was so', 'God saw that it was good', and the refrain 'there was evening and there was morning'.
  • Days 1-3 and 4-6 are often read as parallel (settings and then what fills them); this is a common observation about the structure, not a claim in the text.

Background

  • The word bara ('created') is used in this chapter with God as its only subject.

Where interpreters differ

  • How is the first verse constructed?
    • Main clause: 'In the beginning God created' (traditional reading, reflected in most English translations)
    • Dependent construction: 'When God began to create' / 'In the beginning of God's creating' (Rashi (summary; unchecked))

Context notes for this verse (draft, unreviewed)
  • Rashi (11th c.) on Gen 1:1 — Jewish medieval commentary (needs-source-check): Rashi reads bereshit as in a construct relationship ('in the beginning of God's creating') rather than as an absolute 'in the beginning'. The Hebrew allows both readings because of the vowel pointing's relation to the following verb. Edition and page not verified in this build.
  • John 1:1-3 (New Testament) — Christian reception (needs-source-check): John 1:1 opens with 'In the beginning' and speaks of creation through the Word, widely recognised as echoing Gen 1:1-3. This is a Christian reading of the creation account, distinct from the Hebrew lexical meaning of reshit or davar. Not verified against an edition.

Context notes for this verse (draft, unreviewed)
  • H7307 (draft-unreviewed; contested): ruach is in construct with Elohim and is the subject of a feminine participle (merachefet), matching ruach's usual feminine gender. The BDB outline lists 'spirit of God' and 'wind' as separate senses. Some interpreters read 'ruach Elohim' as the Spirit/breath of God; others read Elohim as an intensifier ('a mighty wind'). The Hebrew words alone do not settle between them.
  • Rashi on Gen 1:2 — Jewish medieval commentary (needs-source-check): Rashi comments on the 'ruach Elohim' that hovered over the water as the 'throne of glory' suspended in the air, an early rabbinic image he reports. Not verified against an edition.
