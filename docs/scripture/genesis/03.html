Context: The nachash, the tree and the consequences (draft, unreviewed)

Literary observations

  • The wordplay arum/arumim connects chapters 2 and 3.
  • The speakers' choice of the divine name changes in the dialogue (the serpent says 'Elohim', the narrator says 'YHWH Elohim'); this is an observable feature of the text.

Background

  • The text does not identify the serpent with any figure other than a creature of the field; later identifications are interpretive traditions.

Now the serpent was more subtle than any animal of the field which Yahweh God had made. He said to the woman, “Has God really said, ‘You shall not eat of any tree of the garden’?”

Context notes for this verse (draft, unreviewed)
  • H5175 (draft-unreviewed): nachash is the ordinary Hebrew word for serpent in the BDB outline. The verse itself calls it a creature of the field that YHWH God made; it does not name any other identity. Later identifications are interpretations, not lexical senses.
  • Rashi on Gen 3:1 — Jewish medieval commentary (needs-source-check): Rashi links arum ('cunning') of the nachash with arumim ('naked') in Gen 2:25, a wordplay present in the Hebrew text itself. Not verified against an edition.
