Context: The human, the garden and the first command (draft, unreviewed)

Literary observations

  • Gen 2:4 introduces the first toledot ('generations/account') formula, which recurs in Genesis as a marker of narrative sections (the formula's occurrences can be found with the lemma index).

Background

  • The ground (adamah), the human (adam) and tilling (avad) are tied together by sound and by the narrative.

Where interpreters differ

  • What does 'le-ovdah u-leshomrah' (2:15) refer to?
    • Care of the garden (common reading)
    • Grammatical antecedent is the feminine 'adamah' (grammatical observation (suffix is feminine; 'gan' is masculine))

Context notes for this verse (draft, unreviewed)
  • H5315 (draft-unreviewed): 'the human became a nefesh chayah' describes the human as a living being, in the same phrase used of animals in Gen 1:20. The text does not say the human 'has' a detachable soul.
  • H120 (draft-unreviewed; contested): 'ha-adam' carries the definite article and is formed from the 'adamah' in the same verse. Whether it means 'the human', 'the first man' or a generic 'earthling' before Eve appears is debated; the article shows it is not yet a personal name here.
  • H127 (draft-unreviewed): 'dust from the adamah': the ground is named as the material of the human. The Hebrew has a sound link between ha-adam and ha-adamah (see the wordplay note).
  • Targum Onkelos on Gen 2:7 — Targum (Aramaic translation tradition) (needs-source-check): Onkelos renders nefesh chayah in a way commonly translated 'a speaking spirit', stressing speech. Not verified against an edition.
  • Rashi on Gen 2:7 — Jewish medieval commentary (needs-source-check): Rashi notes that animals are also called nefesh chayah, and says the human's is distinguished by added knowledge and speech. Not verified against an edition.
