רוּחַ — ruach — Full Hebrew Treatment (Finished, Nov 23, 2026)

Labels follow Aaron's rule: observation / interpretation / hypothesis / declaration are kept separate. Read with the nephesh study: רוּחַ and נֶפֶשׁ are the two breath-words, and the text keeps them distinguishable even where English flattens both into "spirit/soul."

1. The word itself

  • Consonantal text: רוח (resh–vav–ḥet)
  • Pointed text: רוּחַ
  • Transliteration: rûaḥ — "ROO-akh" (final ḥet sounded in the throat)
  • Strong's / lexicon: H7307; TWOT 2131a
  • Part of speech: noun, usually feminine, occasionally masculine (BDB notes the masculine use at Exod 10:13 and elsewhere) — one of the few Hebrew nouns that swings gender, which the versions mostly hide.
  • Frequency: 378 occurrences (BDB head-count; the standard figure quoted across the lexica). BDB's entry helpfully counts its own senses — breath 33×, wind 117×, temper/disposition-spirit 76×, the spirit of the living being 25×, the spirit of God 94× — and those sub-counts are used in §3 with their source named.
  • Forms: absolute רוּחַ; construct identical (רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים, "ruach of God"); suffixed רוּחִי "my ruach" (Gen 6:3), רוּחֲךָ "your ruach" (Ps 104:30); plural רוּחוֹת, "winds/spirits" (Ps 104:4; Num 16:22, "God of the ruchot of all flesh").

2. Root

Strong's: from רוּחַ the verb (H7306), "to breathe, blow" — the noun is moving air, wherever it moves: in the sky, in the chest, in the temper, from God. The lexica treat the connection as straightforward; unlike adam and shem, this word's root is not a fight. A neighbouring derivative matters for §5: רֵיחַ (rêaḥ, "smell, scent," H7381) is listed by the lexica from the same breathing/blowing stock — air carrying something to the nose. Print as lexicon arrangement, not as a sermon.

3. Semantic range (BDB, with BDB's own counts)

  1. Breath of mouth or nostrils (33×) — Job's "my breath is strange to my wife" (19:17); and thinned to nothing: "windy words" (Job 16:3), prophets who "shall become ruach" — mere air (Jer 5:13).
  2. Wind (117×) — the east wind that brings the locusts and the sea-wind that takes them (Exod 10:13, 19); the wind God sends over the flood waters (Gen 8:1); the four winds as the quarters of the sky (Jer 49:36) — hence ruach can even mean a side/direction.
  3. Spirit as temper, disposition, animation (76×) — that which breathes fast when a person is stirred: Pharaoh's ruach troubled (Gen 41:8); "shortness of ruach" for impatience (Exod 6:9); a haughty ruach before a fall (Prov 16:18); the ruach that revives when Jacob hears Joseph lives (Gen 45:27).
  4. The spirit of the living being in flesh, men and animals (25×) — Gen 6:17; 7:15, "all flesh in which is ruach ḥayyim, the breath/spirit of life"; Eccl 3:21 asks who knows whether the ruach of man goes upward and the ruach of the beast down — a question the text leaves open, and this study leaves it where the text did.
  5. The spirit of God (94×) — creating, equipping, prophesying, judging: §4. BDB notes the phrase is absent from Deuteronomy and Jeremiah — a distribution fact worth knowing before anyone builds a doctrine on the phrase's frequency.

The centre: ruach is air in motion with effects. Every sense — weather, breath, mood, God's action — is that one picture at a different scale. English needs three words (wind/breath/spirit) for what Hebrew does with one, and every translation choice in §8 is a loss managed, not a problem solved.

4. Biblical examples — Genesis first

Over the waters — Gen 1:2. וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים מְרַחֶפֶת עַל־פְּנֵי הַמָּיִם — "and ruach elohim was hovering over the face of the waters." The Bible's second verse, and its most argued ruach: "the Spirit of God" (traditional), "a wind from God" (NRSV), "a wind from God sweeping" (NJPS), Speiser's "an awesome wind." The participle מְרַחֶפֶת (məraḥepet, hovering/fluttering) is used again of the eagle over its nest in Deut 32:11 — the text's own second use of a rare word, and the strongest internal clue to the picture. Rabbinic reading (Genesis Rabbah 2) pictures it "like a dove hovering." All of this is printed because the verse will bear weight in Aaron's work: the Hebrew says ruach elohim hovering; everything past that is a translation decision or a tradition, and must be labelled as one or the other.

Not Gen 2:7 — a deliberate distinction. When God breathes life into the adam, the word is נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים (nishmat ḥayyim, "breath of life," from נְשָׁמָה, nəshāmâ), not ruach — and what the man becomes is nephesh. Genesis itself keeps three breath-words in play (ruach, neshamah, nephesh); a study that merges them is studying English, not Hebrew.

The cool of the day — Gen 3:8. The couple hear God walking in the garden לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם — "at the ruach of the day": the day's breeze, the evening wind. Translations split ("cool of the day" traditional). Either way the word is weather before it is theology in this verse.

My ruach shall not abide — Gen 6:3. "My ruach shall not [abide / strive — the verb יָדוֹן is itself disputed] in man forever, for he is flesh; his days shall be 120 years." Two uncertainties in one verse (the verb, and whether 120 years is a lifespan cap or a countdown to the flood) — printed here because the verse is quoted constantly and both disputes are real.

The flood pair — Gen 7:22; 8:1. All in whose nostrils was nishmat-ruach ḥayyim (the two breath-words compounded) died; then God makes a ruach — a wind — pass over the earth, and the waters subside. The same word that is life-breath in the creatures is the weather that saves the remnant. The text does the theology by usage.

A man in whom is ruach elohim — Gen 41:38. Pharaoh on Joseph: the first time the phrase marks a person's equipment — wisdom to read dreams and govern. Compare Bezalel (Exod 31:3), filled with ruach elohim for craftsmanship — building, not prophecy. The ruach equips hands as well as mouths.

The rushing ruach — Judges; 1 Samuel. The ruach of YHWH "rushes upon" Samson and Saul (Judg 14:6; 1 Sam 10:10; 11:6) — sudden, episodic empowerment; and it can depart, and an evil ruach can be sent (1 Sam 16:14) — a verse every honest treatment must include: the text's ruach-world includes a harmful spirit "from YHWH," and interpretations of that (permission, idiom for melancholy, later theology) are interpretations, labelled.

Return — Eccl 12:7; Ps 104:29–30. "The dust returns to the earth as it was, and the ruach returns to God who gave it." Psalm 104 sets the rhythm for every creature: "You take away their ruach, they die and return to dust; you send forth your ruach, they are created." Ruach here is God's to give and recall — the creature holds it on loan. This is the closest the Hebrew Bible comes to a ruach doctrine, and it is stated as poetry about all flesh, not as a definition.

The valley — Ezek 37:1–14. The Bible's great ruach wordplay, and it only works in Hebrew: the prophet is told to prophesy to the ruach — "come from the four ruchot (winds), O ruach, and breathe on these slain" — breath, wind, and Spirit in one word in one breath, v. 9. English must pick three words and lose the chord. If Aaron teaches one passage to show why Hebrew thought must be met in Hebrew, this is the strongest candidate in this file.

Not by might — Zech 4:6; Joel 3:1 [Eng. 2:28]. "Not by might, nor by power, but by my ruach." And the promise: "I will pour out my ruach on all flesh" — the phrase's widest horizon in the Hebrew text.

5. Wordplay

  • Ezekiel 37's triple sense — §4: the passage is the wordplay; no other passage in the Bible leans on all three senses at once.
  • ruach / reach (scent) — Gen 8:21: God smells (וַיָּרַח, vayyāraḥ, from the same stock) the soothing scent of Noah's offering, in the same chapter where his ruach-wind dries the earth. The narrator's air-words cluster around the flood's turn. Lexicon-supported kinship; narrative observation ours, labelled.
  • merachefet — hovering: Gen 1:2 and Deut 32:11 only (the verb also at Jer 23:9 in a different sense, "shake"). Two-text wordplay across the Torah's frame: the ruach hovers at creation; God hovers over Israel in the wilderness song. The echo is in the text.

6. Semitic cognates — where justified

  • Aramaic רוּחַ (rûḥā) — wind, spirit; the Targums' and Daniel's word (Dan 2:35 the wind; 5:12 the spirit in Daniel).
  • Arabic rūḥ — spirit, soul; and rīḥ, "wind" — Arabic keeps the wind/spirit split in two related forms, showing the same root doing both jobs the Hebrew does with one.
  • Ugaritic rḥ — attested in the wind/breath field; cited at the confidence the fragmentary corpus allows.
  • Caution: Akkadian has no common everyday cognate doing this work (its šāru, "wind," is a different word) — a reminder that cognate maps have coasts, and this word's family is Northwest Semitic and Arabic, not universal.

7. Ancient context and traditions

  • Breath-of-life is the ancient world's common anthropology — Egyptian and Mesopotamian texts likewise animate the formed figure by divine breath or a god's life-substance. Genesis's version is distinguished by the loan-structure of Ps 104 (§4): the ruach is God's, lent to all flesh alike, recalled at death. Parallel noted; dependence not claimed.
  • "Holy Spirit" — רוּחַ קָדְשְׁךָ / רוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ occurs only three times in the Hebrew Bible (Ps 51:13 [Eng. 51:11]; Isa 63:10, 11). The familiar phrase is mostly a later — rabbinic and New Testament — development. Any treatment that reads the later doctrine back into every ruach elohim is adding, and must say so.
  • New Testament: the Greek word is πνεῦμα (pneuma) — Greek first, per Aaron's rule: pneuma carries its own range (wind, breath, spirit; John 3:8 plays pneuma-as-wind exactly as Ezekiel plays ruach), and the Hebrew background illuminates it without replacing it. No "original Hebrew" wording is claimed for any NT passage.

8. Translation alternatives

  • "wind" — where weather or direction is in view (Gen 8:1; Exod 10). Untranslatable as anything else there.
  • "breath" — where the chest is in view (Job 19:17; Ps 104:29).
  • "spirit / Spirit" — where temper, endowment, or God's action is in view; the capital S is a theological decision by the translator, invisible in Hebrew, which has no capitals. Say so when it matters — at Gen 1:2 it matters most.
  • "mind / temper / courage" — KJV already reaches for these (Gen 41:8 "troubled in mind" territory; Josh 2:11 "courage"). Ruach's range forces English to scatter; a good translation keeps a footnote trail so the reader can reassemble the one word.

9. Uncertainties, printed

  1. Gen 1:2 — Spirit, wind from God, or mighty wind: the Hebrew permits the argument; it does not settle it. The hovering participle and Deut 32:11 are evidence for the picture, not for one English noun.
  2. Gen 6:3 — the verb יָדוֹן and the 120 years: both disputed.
  3. Gen 3:8 — "cool of the day" is an inference from the breeze, not the word's gloss.
  4. Eccl 3:21 — the upward/downward question about human and animal ruach is posed by the text and left open by it. Closed readings are additions.
  5. The gender swing of the noun — real, unexplained by any consensus, and not a doctrine.

Answer from memory first, then open each one to check. Drawn from this study’s own text.

What are BDB’s own counts of ruach’s senses?

Of 378 occurrences: wind 117×, temper/disposition 76×, the spirit of God 94×, breath 33×, the spirit of the living being 25×.

Is ruach’s root disputed?

No — from the verb “to breathe, blow.” The noun is moving air, wherever it moves: in the sky, in the chest, in the temper, from God.

What is unusual about ruach’s gender?

It is usually feminine but occasionally masculine (for example Exod 10:13) — one of the few Hebrew nouns that swings gender.
