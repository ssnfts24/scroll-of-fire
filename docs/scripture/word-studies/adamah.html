אֲדָמָה — adamah — Full Hebrew Treatment (Finished, Nov 23, 2026)

Labels follow Aaron's rule: observation / interpretation / hypothesis / declaration are kept separate. This is the ground-word of the adam cluster — read it with אָדָם (adam, the human) and אֱנוֹשׁ (enosh, the mortal).

1. The word itself

  • Consonantal text: אדמה (aleph–dalet–mem–he)
  • Pointed text: אֲדָמָה
  • Transliteration: ʾăḏāmâ — "ah-dah-MAH"
  • Strong's / lexicon: H127
  • Part of speech: feminine noun
  • Frequency: BDB's entry is headed 224; the KJV concordance count is 225. Quote as "about 225," method named — the one-occurrence difference is a counting-method artefact, and this study will not pretend to know which artefact.
  • Forms: construct אַדְמַת (ʾaḏmaṯ, "ground/land of —"), e.g. אַדְמַת מִצְרַיִם, "the land of Egypt" (Gen 47:20); suffixed אַדְמָתִי, "my ground" (Job 31:38); plural אֲדָמוֹת (Ps 49:12 [Eng. 49:11]) — the plural is rare, because the word usually names the one ground under your feet, not grounds in general.

2. Root — and an honest warning about direction

Strong's derives adamah "from H119" (אָדַם, "to be red"): "soil, from its general redness." BDB is visibly less sure: its entry floats the tilled-ground picture, then reports a comparison with Arabic ʾadama, "skin" — the ground as the earth's skin, smoothly covering and close-fitting — and a possible root דם with an Arabic verb "to smear, spread over a surface."

The warning: even the direction of derivation inside this family is disputed. Does adam come from adamah (the human named from his ground), does adamah come from adam, or do both hang on the red-root? The lexica do not settle it, and the famous Gen 2:7 wordplay works in the text whichever way the history ran. Preachers pick a direction; this study prints the dispute.

  • Observation: Gen 2:7 and 3:19 tie adam to adamah in the text itself. That is a textual fact, independent of etymology.
  • Do not upgrade: "adamah means red soil" is Strong's gloss, not a consensus. Red soils exist in the land; the word's meaning in use is ground/land, full stop (§3).

3. Semantic range (BDB)

  1. Ground as tilled, yielding sustenance — the farmer's ground: Gen 2:5, 9; 4:2–3; Deut 28 passim; Prov 12:11, "he who works his adamah will have plenty of bread."
  2. A piece of ground, landed property — Gen 47:18–26, where Egypt's adamah is bought, sold, and taxed; Ps 49:12's plural, men naming "their adamot" after themselves (a shem-theme: see the shem study).
  3. Earth as material substance — what things are made of: Gen 2:7 (the human), 2:19 (the animals), Exod 20:24 (an altar of adamah), Isa 45:9 (the clay arguing with the potter).
  4. Ground as the earth's visible surface — Gen 1:25; 6:20; the face of the adamah, פְּנֵי הָאֲדָמָה, a fixed phrase: what the flood covers (Gen 7:23), what man is driven from (Gen 4:14).
  5. Land, territory, country — approaching אֶרֶץ (ʾereṣ): "the adamah of Judah" (Isa 19:17), and above all Ezekiel's אַדְמַת יִשְׂרָאֵל, "the land of Israel" (16+ times in Ezekiel) — the land as promised and given.
  6. The whole inhabited earth — rare and elevated: Gen 12:3 and 28:14, "all the families of the adamah" (see §4).

The centre of gravity: adamah is worked, owned, promised earth — nearer and more handled than the cosmic אֶרֶץ. It is the ground in relation to the human who stands on it, tills it, is made of it, and is buried in it.

4. Biblical examples — the curse-and-lifting arc, Genesis first

Before the human — Gen 2:5. "No shrub of the field was yet in the land, and no plant had yet sprung up... and there was no adam to work the adamah." The pair is introduced as a pair, two verses before the forming. The text wants the words heard together.

The forming — Gen 2:7. Dust (עָפָר) of the adamah is the raw material. Note the precision: adam is not formed from adamah as a block; he is dust of it — the ground at its finest and loosest. The wordplay is adam/adamah; the material word is ʿāpār, dust. Keep the three words distinct; sermons often smear them.

The curse on the ground — Gen 3:17–19, 23. "Cursed is the adamah because of you" — the ground itself takes the sentence: thorns, sweat, bread won hard. The man is sent out "to work the adamah from which he was taken" (3:23). Work is not the curse; resistant ground is.

Blood in the ground — Gen 4:2–3, 10–12. Cain is עֹבֵד אֲדָמָה, a worker of the ground, who brings its fruit. Then: "the voice of your brother's blood cries to me from the adamah, which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood." The ground is a witness and a mouth. Cain's sentence is measured in ground: "the adamah will no longer yield its strength to you." This is the adamah as a moral party to the story, not scenery — and the first blood in the ground is the cluster's adam/dam echo made narrative (see the adam study §5).

The comfort promised — Gen 5:29. Noah is named with a gloss the text does give: "This one will comfort us concerning our work and the toil of our hands, from the adamah that YHWH has cursed." The genealogy chapter carries the ground's story inside the names.

The curse lifted — Gen 8:21. After the flood: "I will never again curse the adamah for man's sake." The arc 3:17 → 5:29 → 8:21 is the adamah's own storyline across the primeval history, and it closes. What does not close: the return — "to dust you shall return" stands.

The husbandman — Gen 9:20. Noah, אִישׁ הָאֲדָמָה, "man of the adamah," plants a vineyard. The phrase honours the worker of the ground; 2 Chron 26:10 calls Uzziah's servants lovers of the adamah, and Zech 13:5 has a man disclaim prophecy with "I am a worker of the adamah."

All the families of the adamah — Gen 12:3; 28:14. The blessing of Abram and of Jacob is measured against the adamah's whole population: "in you all the families of the adamah shall be blessed." The ground-word at its widest reach — from one man's plot to every family on the ground.

The promised land — Exod 20:12; Ezekiel passim. "That your days may be long on the adamah YHWH your God gives you." In Ezekiel the phrase אַדְמַת יִשְׂרָאֵל becomes the land's covenant name, spoken over mountains and exiles. Adamah here is not soil chemistry; it is inheritance.

5. Wordplay

  • adam / adamah — the cluster's master pun (Gen 2:5, 7; 3:19, 23).
  • adamah / dam — the blood that cries from the adamah (Gen 4:10) puts דָּם inside the אֲדָמָה sentence, as Gen 9:5–6 puts it inside the adam sentence. The ground receives the blood; the sounds say the two belong to one story. Textual echo, yes; signed etymology, no.
  • The ground opens its mouth (Gen 4:11) — adamah personified. Job 31:38 plays it again from the other side: "if my adamah cried out against me" — the ground as plaintiff. The personification is a pattern, not a one-off.

6. Semitic cognates — where justified

  • Arabic ʾadama — "skin, surface" (BDB's comparison, via Fleischer): the ground as the earth's skin. A comparison, printed as one.
  • South Arabian / Sabaean — the root family is old in the peninsula (see the adam study on ʾdm), but a direct adamah cognate with this exact sense is not cleanly attested; do not manufacture one.
  • Caution: adamah is the least cognate-supported word in this cluster. Its meaning is carried by its 224 biblical uses, not by relatives abroad.

7. Ancient context

  • Humans formed from earth/clay is the ancient world's shared picture (Atrahasis; Egyptian Khnum shaping man on the potter's wheel). Genesis's adamah stands in that world; its distinctives are the breath of Gen 2:7 and the moral storyline of §4 — the ground can be cursed, can witness, can be comforted-for, can be promised. In the neighbouring epics the ground is material; in Genesis it is also a party.
  • Text vs. tradition: later Jewish tradition gathers dust "from the four corners of the earth" for Adam's forming (Genesis Rabbah) — a homily on why the whole earth can receive his descendants' dead. Labelled tradition; the text says only "dust of the adamah."

8. Translation alternatives

  • "ground" — best for the Genesis cluster and the farming texts; keeps the adam echo audible.
  • "land" — right for the territory sense (Ezekiel; Gen 47), wrong for Gen 2:7, where "land" loses the handful-of-soil scale.
  • "soil / earth" — acceptable for the material sense (Exod 20:24); "earth" risks confusion with אֶרֶץ and with the planet.
  • "the land" as inheritance — in the promise texts, some translations keep adamah as "land" deliberately; the choice should be visible, not silent.

9. Uncertainties, printed

  1. Direction of derivation in the adam/adamah/red family — unsettled (§2). The wordplay is certain; the history is not.
  2. "Redness" as the word's origin — Strong's gloss; BDB unconvinced.
  3. The Arabic "skin" comparison — suggestive, unproven.
  4. The 224 vs. 225 count — method artefact, unresolved and minor.
