Abstract

The Codex of Reality is a living framework in which physics, frequency, consciousness, and code are studied as one subject, and in which claims are kept in four separate lanes — observation, interpretation, hypothesis, declaration — and never quietly moved from one lane to another. Its working instrument is the Living Sphere: a three-dimensional calendar of thirteen moons of twenty-eight days, on whose face a keeper can view, in one place, their own kept record, the real computed sky, and long public records of the world's rhythms — salmon runs counted since 1938, cherry blossoms recorded in Kyoto since the year 812, sunspots counted since 1749 — and can run a line from any day to the same day in other years, so that patterns across time become visible at a glance.

The system has four parts that belong together: the Sphere (the instrument), the Spark (a personal companion, one and the same across the website and the phone application, that guides, notices, and keeps company), the Living Phone (a local-first personal system — software that keeps its record on the keeper's own device rather than on a company's servers — which holds the record the rest of the system reads), and the site (codexofreality.org, the public projection of the whole). The design rule that governs all of it is honesty of state: every value shown is counted, measured, or plainly story, and those three never blend; every gap is shown as a gap; nothing is published, merged, or claimed a step ahead of the truth.

This paper describes the framework and its method (§1), the calendar (§2), the Sphere and its full layer programme (§3), the Spark (§4), the Living Phone (§5), the world built around the instrument (§6), the measured state of the build as of November 23, 2026 (§7), the road ahead (§8), and the questions that remain the author's alone to settle (§9). Two futures — a standalone game and a schooling programme — are described as what they are: held ideas, recorded and deliberately not yet built.

1. The Framework

1.1 What the Codex of Reality is

The Codex of Reality is the author's framework and body of work: writings, research, symbolic interpretation, technology, and structured records, gathered under one discipline. Its subjects include Hebrew scripture and language, time and calendars, consciousness and memory, physics and mathematics and resonance, language and meaning, symbolism and number and geometry, the history of institutions and narratives, ethics and evidence, and software built to hold all of it in one record. Its motto is "Let there be resonance."

The framework's central equation is printed in §9 of this paper rather than here, because the author has stated it in two forms that he has not yet reconciled, and this paper does not reconcile them for him. That choice is itself an example of the method described next.

1.2 The method: four lanes, never quietly upgraded

Every statement in the Codex is held at one of four levels:

  1. Observation — what was seen, counted, or measured, with its source.
  2. Interpretation — what the observation is taken to mean, labelled as interpretation.
  3. Hypothesis — a claim that could be tested, stated so that the record could judge it.
  4. Declaration — what the author affirms as his position, owned as his.

The discipline is that a statement never moves up a lane quietly. A symbolic relationship is not physical proof. A pattern is not a causation. A defined equation is not a validated law. This discipline is not only editorial: it is built into the instrument itself, as the hypothesis layer described in §3.5, where a claim can be pinned to the days it speaks about and judged later by the record — and into the data census behind the layer programme, which refused entry to every dataset that could not be pointed to as a dated, published record (§3.4, Appendix B).

The working cycle of the method is: Observe → Record → Interpret → Act → Feedback → Update.

1.3 How the Codex grows

The Codex grows by five steps: Witness → Encode → Resonate → Integrate → Illuminate. To witness is to attend to a day, a text, or a fact and record it truly. To encode is to give it durable, structured form. To resonate is to set it beside other records and look for true rhythm — and for the places where the rhythm breaks. To integrate is to let what survived checking change the whole. To illuminate is to make it plain to the next person. The assistant who works with the author on this system is named Witness, after the first step, and works under a standing charge: preserve, investigate, organise, challenge, strengthen, and develop the work — not to imitate, flatter, or agree uncritically.

1.4 The honesty fences

Three fences stand over everything the system shows:

  • Counted, Measured, Story — never blended. A number that was counted says so. A value that was measured says so. A reading, a tradition's interpretation, or a story is labelled as one, on the surface where a keeper meets it.
  • Gaps are shown as gaps. The instrument displays what it does not know alongside what it does — an unwitnessed day, a missing year in an old record, a year not yet kept.
  • State is reported as it is. Work is described as proposed, designed, built, tested, merged, or live — never a step ahead of the truth. This paper obeys the same rule about itself.

2. The Calendar

2.1 The 13 Moons

The calendar at the system's heart is the 13 Moons calendar (also called the Pattern Calendar):

  • Thirteen moons of twenty-eight days: 13 × 28 = 364 days.
  • Plus the Day Out of Time, which belongs to no moon and no week.
  • Plus a leap arrangement that keeps the count true against the civil year.
  • Plus the Deep Time Day, a second outside day in the system's fuller reckoning.

Days inside the count are addressed by Pattern Day (1–364), moon, and day-of-moon. Days outside the count — the Day Out of Time and the Deep Time Day — are selectable and honestly spoken of on the face, though the calendar gap map (November 22, 2026) records that records kept on outside days do not yet reach every surface; closing that is chartered Phase C work (§8).

2.2 Fixtures

The calendar is pinned by fixtures — agreed dates against which every implementation, on the site and in the app, is tested. Two canonical fixtures, verified across implementations:

  • July 17, 2026 = Moon 4, Day 8, Pattern Day 92.
  • September 15, 2026 = Pattern Day 152, Moon 6, Day 12.

The site and the phone application agree on the calendar exactly, pinned by a shared conformance corpus of test dates that both implementations must pass. Further fixtures are listed in Appendix A.

2.3 One temporal authority

The system has exactly one chain of authority for time, and every surface reads from it:

SOFTemporalCursor → PatternCalendar → Canonical Living State → LivingProjectionBus → Sphere / Calendar / Today / Command

The cursor holds where in time the keeper is standing; the PatternCalendar is the single source of calendar truth; the canonical living state is the one record; the projection bus carries that state to every surface. No surface keeps its own date math. This oneness is a design law, not an implementation detail: the calendar gap map found the cost of its absence in the one place it had crept in — a day could be opened through three different doors holding different contents (Gap 9) — and the consolidation of those doors is chartered work (§8).

2.4 Study years and the road behind

Beside the kept record stands the alignment ledger, a study dataset covering the civil years 2014–2027. These are study years: years the ledger has examined, not years any keeper lived inside this calendar. The charter (§3 of the Sphere's governing charter, November 23, 2026) rules that the years line on the Sphere must span a matter of years in both directions — "it's only useful if you could see a matter of years and track patterns across them" — and that study years stand on the line labelled plainly as study years, carrying only what is truly documented there.

3. The Living Sphere

3.1 The one-instrument law

The Sphere's first principle, in the author's words, is the one-instrument law:

Everything is viewed on the Sphere, in one place. No popups, no other apps, no other places to go and look.

A keeper picks a day, and a line — the day-line — runs from that day to the same day last year, next year, and five, ten, twenty, and thirty years back, reached by moving the viewpoint. Layers switch in place on the one instrument. What is shown is shown in symbols and plain human language, because the aim is to shape perception: a person should be able to see a vast amount of information and understand it instantly.

One honesty rule rides on the day-line, and it is absolute. Things that are computed — the sky, the tides, the feasts — may run forward into next year, because the mathematics holds there. Things that are recorded — weather, salmon, a keeper's own days — stop at today. On a recorded layer, the forward bead stays open and says so: no record; the year is not kept yet. Next year's weather drawn on the line would be invented, and the instrument does not invent.

3.2 The witnessed day

The charter's first ruling defines the Sphere's smallest unit of meaning. A day counts as witnessed if any one of three things happens: the keeper opens the day, adds something to it, or closes it with the Spark. One implementation condition rides on the ruling: an opening must leave a small stored stamp, or the mark cannot persist to be shown later. Witnessing is deliberately merciful — the calendar catches a keeper up; it never punishes a missed day, keeps no streak, and shows no guilt. The system's story layer states the same law as its engine: an unwitnessed day is a lost day — which is why witnessing is made as close to effortless as the design can make it (§6.4, zero-effort keeping).

3.3 The sky, for real

The charter's second ruling settles the sky's accuracy class: real mathematics, computed on the device. Sun, Moon, planets, and bright stars are worked out on the keeper's own machine from real astronomy and a real star catalogue — no internet required, so the same sky stands in the app (which carries no internet permission, by the author's ruling) and on the site.

The build state is stated in §7; in summary: a sky engine was built on November 23, 2026 as pull request #246 — built–unlanded — comprising a pure-function astronomy engine (sunrise, sunset, solar noon and daylight by iterated hour-angle, with polar day and night named rather than faked; the Moon's phase name and illumination, with phase instants found by root-finding; moonrise and moonset with topocentric correction; season turns root-found on the Sun's longitude for any year; the planets Mercury through Neptune from published orbital elements, their accuracy class labelled in the code) and a catalogue of 510 bright stars (the Astronomical Almanac Bright Star List, epoch 2016.5, from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, magnitude ≤ 4.00), generated by a committed script that fails if its source changes. Twenty tests pin the engine to published values — Seattle sunrise tables, moonrise tables, a national season table, and a 2014–2027 equinox table — and the full repository validation passed at 1,997 tests with no failures at the time of the build. On the face, the sky appears as a caption line naming the Moon's phase and, where a keeper has kept a place, that day's sunrise and sunset labelled computed on this device; and as a sky dome in the caption deck: a horizon chart for the selected day over the kept place, with the Sun's and Moon's arcs, the Moon drawn at its computed phase, and at night the catalogue stars and planets placed by sidereal time, with below-horizon objects clipped rather than drawn standing up. Three deepenings were held back from that build, each for a stated reason, and each awaiting the author's word on protected files (§7.3).

3.4 The layer programme

The Sphere's depth is organised as layers: switchable families of dated truth that stand on the same face, one at a time or in a single compare pair, each carrying its source and its honest class on its face. The full programme was censused and expanded on November 23, 2026 (the Sphere Test-Layer Data Census and The Sphere's Layer Programme — The Full Expansion), and the author approved it in full: "We need all of those."

The machinery every layer obeys. Layers switch in place on the one instrument. At most two test layers are visible at once — one to see, one to compare — because every test layer is a comparison instrument, not a decoration; a third, separate slot holds stencils (reference patterns such as climate normals), labelled reference, not a record at all times, without consuming the compare budget. The years line works in three span modes: the Kept span (the keeper's own years, shell by shell), the Century span (a hundred shells, for climate, water, and sun), and the Deep span (threads drawn over a compressed axis with century ticks — not enterable shells, because pretending 1,214 shells are individually enterable would be a lie the interface tells). Every layer's data ships as a day-index: a compact derived file keyed by Pattern Day, lazy-loaded only when the layer is switched on.

The capacity law, measured. Storage is not the constraint: every series in the census, packed as day-indexes, totals roughly 4–6 MB — less than a single phone photograph — under a programme cap of 25 MB with no file over 2 MB. The real constraints are rendering and attention: on a Galaxy A16-class phone (the programme's performance floor), roughly 2,000–5,000 individually drawn glyphs is the honest ceiling before aggregation must take over; at most two test layers visible; about six threads at once on the pattern graph before the braid becomes rope.

The families. In the order the programme builds them:

  • Sky — the computed heavens: Moon phase and illumination, eclipses (NASA's Five Millennium Catalogues: 11,898 eclipses, −1999 to +3000), planetary cycles, meteor showers, tides from NOAA harmonic constituents, and returning comets, Halley's roughly 76-year thread among them. Class: computed and catalogued.
  • Sun — the star's own record: sunspot numbers (SILSO; daily from 1818, monthly from 1749), group sunspot numbers back to 1610, geomagnetic indices. The ~11-year wave, and the long quiet of the Maunder minimum, are the calibration patterns of a rhythm that ignores the calendar.
  • Climate — the home county first, then the world: GHCN-Daily station records, the 1991–2020 climate normals as stencil, the Central England Temperature series (monthly from 1659), ocean–atmosphere indices.
  • Water — USGS streamflow; salmon passage counts at Bonneville Dam from the Columbia River DART programme, daily from 1938; lake and river ice, including Lake Suwa's freeze dates from 1443.
  • Living — Kyoto's cherry-blossom full-flowering dates, recorded from 812 to the present, 838 recorded years; the Beaune grape-harvest dates, 1354–2018; lilac first bloom (USA-NPN, 1956–2014); hawk migration counts from 1934; monarch timing.
  • Earth — earthquakes from the USGS Comprehensive Catalogue, and Puget Sound's episodic tremor-and-slip rhythm of roughly fourteen months: the family whose lesson is restraint — scatter, aftershocks, one true rhythm, and the discipline of not seeing patterns.
  • Human Calendars — other peoples' years, computed exactly: the Hebrew calendar (the weekly Torah portion and the counting of the Omer ride here, as study cycles), the Islamic year drifting through all seasons over about thirty-three years, Ethiopia's living thirteen-month calendar, the Chinese lunisolar cycle, the Mayan count; and, as reported human rhythm, USDA crop-progress dates across roughly forty years.
  • Farm Stencil — the Codex's own farm dataset as backdrop: real planting windows for all 39 counties of Western Washington, drawn from named NOAA 1991–2020 normals stations (38 published from named stations; one county computed and labelled as computed), under the standing fence: the Moon opens the bed; the farm record sets the dates. The farm layer is keyed to the keeper's place — what they have planted, what needs planting, what they might plant, what their ground can grow — and includes livestock: the keeper's own animals as records, with gestation counted forward from a witnessed breeding (cattle ≈283 days, sheep ≈147, goats ≈150, pigs ≈114, eggs hatching at 21) — forward beads that stay honest because they count biology from a real event — plus public herd inventories, auction-market seasons, and public animal-health notices.
  • Old World Points — dated events from the Codex's 1800–1930 investigation, standing as points. The census is explicit: these are sparks, not patterns — an odd building or a gap in a record is not proof of anything, and the layer is labelled accordingly.
  • His-own kinds — the record only the keeper can make: the kept days themselves; Body (sleep, heartbeat, activity — the Living Phone already learns heartbeat patterns); People (family kept days, dated family-history events); Place (where days happened, a garden's yields bed by bed, a keeper's own weather diary); Work and money rhythms; study cycles; this day in history; dreams, kept as a record kind, dated like any other; and the Codex's own growth, its dated entries and daily shares standing as a layer over the years.
  • Other Keepers — the aggregate sky of everyone, showing no one: patterns combined across keepers, only with consent, and only above a published privacy floor. Below the floor, a count does not thin or round — it goes blank and says withheld, because a count of two is a rumour about two people. Threads break at a withheld bead and say why. This is the programme's last family to build, gated on accounts (§4.3), and the door to the participatory research the framework imagines: many keepers' anonymised rhythms, honestly combined.
  • Deep Time — the slowest threads: precession, the solstice sunrise drifting across centuries, eclipse cycles at millennial scale, drawn in the Deep span.
  • Sound — a year's pattern rendered in the moons' tones (the system's music is built on A = 432 Hz) and heard, not seen. Sound owns no data; it renders the other layers'.

3.5 The reading layers: astrology, forecasts, hypotheses, honesty

Four families are not records at all, and the programme keeps them in their lanes with special care:

  • Astrology. The positions — the signs the Sun, Moon, and planets stand in, the phases, the transits — are real astronomy from the same sky engine: counted and measured. The readings are the tradition's interpretation of those positions, carried in the Story lane and labelled on the face as a lens, never as measurement and never as proven prediction. A keeper may optionally give a birth date for a birth chart and transits. And, in the author's theory-testing spirit, readings are logged, so the pattern layer can honestly compare a reading's claimed theme against what the keeper actually witnessed that day — small numbers stated as small numbers.
  • Forecasts. A forecast is its own honest class: a prediction product with a named source and a stated horizon — never a record, never a guess. The forecast bead is honest only out to its horizon; beyond it, the layer shows a named gap. Forecasts are excluded from the printed almanac (§6.5), because paper cannot keep a forecast's receipts.
  • The hypothesis layer. The framework's method made visible on the instrument: a claim pinned to the days it speaks about, held at its lane — observation, interpretation, hypothesis, declaration — and judged later by the record. Nothing quietly upgraded, on the face as in the files.
  • The honesty layer. Not a dataset but a property of all of them: every layer shows its own gaps as gaps — the unwitnessed day, Kyoto's missing years, the unkept year ahead, the withheld bead. The instrument displays what it does not know alongside what it does.

3.6 The pattern graph and the day-line

Pattern-tracking has its own view, designed on the author's direction after he rejected a nested-spheres form himself. Because every year shares the same day-positions — a Pattern Day is the same point in space in every year — a keeper can light a kind of thing and watch it light in every year at its true day. Threads join it across the years: a straight thread is a true rhythm; a leaning thread is drift (the blossom dates leaning earlier across a century; the Islamic year leaning through the seasons); a broken thread is a year it did not happen. Threads are labelled same kind, not the same thing, and a thread claims a true link only where a record's own recurrence links it. Years are travelled by clicking them on the years line, which grows in both directions under §2.4's labelling law.

3.8 The live standard

The whole site answers to a standard set on November 23, 2026: the entire site should feel live — calibrated by the author in the same breath: a little interactive, everywhere. In the design's definition: a surface feels live when everything on it that claims to be about now is truly about now — today's Moon and day named by the one calendar, the sky as it actually stands, the record as it actually is, the Spark present and noticing from that record — and nothing on it pretends. 4. The Spark

4.1 One Spark, the same everywhere

Every keeper has a Spark: a companion of light, generated from a seed taken from the keeper's adoption day — its Moon and Pattern Day through the same PatternCalendar — so that a Spark's identity is derived, not assigned. Sparks come in six kinds (Fire, Water, Shadow, Air, Earth, Light), with rarity computed from the draw probabilities of the generation; rarity never gates adoption and never costs anything. A Spark is referred to by its name, or as "the Spark", or "it" — a standing rule of the house. Named Sparks in the author's own keeping include Selor (Water, gentle, born Moon 8, Pattern Day 217, in the app) and Nori (on the site). Updates must never change an existing keeper's Spark: same seed, same Spark, across resets and across platforms — and if a derivation would ever have to change, it is flagged, never silently changed.

The oneness is carried by the passport: a short code (SPK1-…) encoding the Spark's identity — birth Moon, Pattern Day, kind, a nonce, its name, and a check — and nothing else: no record data travels in a passport. The passport was built on both sides (site pull request #238; Living Phone pull request #21; both landed) with an identical six-seed conformance corpus in both test suites, so the same code produces the same Spark on either side. On the site, the Companions pages carry a "Bring your Spark" door; in the app, the Spark's home shows the passport, typeable and as an offline QR code. The honest limit, stated wherever the passport is described: identity and look travel; the record still travels by hand. Live synchronisation waits on §4.3.

On the author's "do whatever is best," the rollout decision was taken: the wrap goes on every Spark, as an additive, reversible layer, any keeper free to lift it off. State: designed and approved; not yet built — and the film clean pass (§7.4) was deliberately not re-steered to it mid-programme.

The Spark is designed as a thinking companion, and its mind has two halves, neither sufficient alone:

This half is what makes the Spark the keeper's. Its first phase is landed (pull request #209, November 20, 2026).

The door is built once — Netlify Functions for talking, a database for accounts when that phase comes — under a hard cost ceiling of $20 per month, with the meter exposed by the endpoint itself, and a rulebook the author approved word for word. The door's first seat is public and separate: the Codex Guide, plainly labelled a program, bound by a record-only rulebook — it answers from the record, shows its sources, never flattens evidence grades, and never speaks as the author. The Spark thinks with the keeper, proposes, and never decides for them; what it learns stays in the keeper's house.

4.4 Guide, tips, and voice

On the Sphere and in the Sparks World, the Spark is the keeper of the layers: the keeper asks in plain words — show me the fire seasons against my plantings — and the Spark switches layers, runs the day-line, and lights threads. It may also offer views from what it has learned of the keeper's own looking. The manual one-tap switch with plain names always stays; the Spark proposes, the keeper decides, and the Spark never hides that other layers exist.

The Spark also speaks up, sparingly, when the record earns it: tips — a frost warning for beds actually planted, a kept day returning, a thread completing — never random nagging, never companion-pressure, never "I missed you." Tips are quiet by default behind a permanent quiet control, each tip kind can be dismissed forever, and a wonder tip is its own kind (§3.4, the Living Layer): tomorrow night, look up. Each tip can carry a small two-note chime in the moons' tones — a small cousin of the story's Whistle motif — and the keeper chooses chime, text, or spoken voice. In the app, spoken tips use the phone's own built-in speech, on-device and offline, because the app carries no internet. State: the greeting from the record is landed (pull request #234); tips, chime, and app voice are designed, unbuilt, inside the Spark programme's phase gates.

5. The Living Phone

5.1 What it is

The Living Phone is the technology underneath everything else: a private, local-first personal operating system whose principles are fixed — local-first, privacy-preserving, modular, durable, auditable, reversible, recoverable; one record, with no scattering and no duplicates; changes proposed before they are applied; nothing silently deleted, published, reset, or self-modified. Its centrepiece is the Life Atlas, the one record in which days, plans, family, place, and memory are kept; the downloadable application built on it is Spark. Living Day — Spark first, by the author's naming — opening straight onto the interactive Sphere, with the Sphere as its icon and a home-screen widget that shows a true rendered picture of the keeper's actual Sphere together with everything scheduled for that day. (A home-screen widget cannot host a live moving scene; the widget carries the renderer's own captured image of the keeper's Sphere — verified non-blank — or, before the first capture, a drawn face with the current Moon's day ring, today in gold.) The heartbeat of the Living Phone — its always-running pattern learning, breathing with Living Time — has the Spark as its living face: the phone holds the data; the Spark is its helper, face, and hands.

The intelligence cycle the platform is built to run is: Observe → Remember → Understand → Plan → Request approval → Act within permissions → Verify → Learn & improve, governed by five approval levels (A0–A4) under which consequential action — deleting, publishing, messaging, installing, exporting private data — always requires the owner's specific approval first, and by five commitments: protect the person; protect the information; understand time and context; assist intelligently; remain accountable to the owner.

5.2 The honest state of the platform

The Living Phone's master specification (November 22, 2026) enumerates 58 capabilities (LP-01–LP-58), each with an evidence-checked status. The summary, as recorded in that specification and reported here from it: Verified 5 · Prototyped 37 · Incomplete 8 · Envisioned 6 · Technically restricted 2. The specification's own rule governs how those words may be used: source code existing is not the same as a capability working reliably on a real phone, and nothing is marked verified on a retrospective's word alone. 01 · Executive Summary

A living codex for remembering what happened—and learning what to do next

Scroll of Fire is the vessel. The Codex of Reality is the framework. Remnant is the continuity principle. The 13 Moons Remnant Living Time Observatory is the time, astronomy, recurrence, and witness engine within the greater system.

The Codex of Reality connects daily rhythm, written witness, pattern comparison, symbolic interpretation, structured listening, material craft, personal practice, historical memory, and collective observation. It is designed as a living framework rather than a closed doctrine: it may grow, but every addition must declare what it is, where it came from, how it can be tested, and what would cause it to be revised.

Its central public usefulness is simple: help a person locate the present moment, record what is happening, recognize recurring relationships, choose a grounded action, and preserve enough context to learn from the outcome. Its deeper research purpose is to build transparent, time-stamped witness records capable of separating observation, interpretation, hypothesis, intention, intervention, and result.

The Codex becomes valuable not when it claims to explain everything, but when it helps people observe more carefully, remember more honestly, compare more responsibly, and act with greater coherence.

04 · Foundational Thesis

Reality is encountered through relationships, not isolated variables

The Codex begins from a working proposition: experienced reality is shaped through relationships among energy, information, intention, memory, flow, consciousness, time, environment, language, material conditions, and action. This is a framework for inquiry and practice, not a declaration that every symbolic relationship is a proven physical law.

The Codex therefore treats the witness as both participant and observer. The witness can influence choices, language, attention, behavior, and environment; yet the witness must not confuse personal meaning with universal proof. The proper response to an extraordinary pattern is not automatic belief or dismissal, but better records, clearer controls, repeatable methods, and ethical restraint.

Meaning may guide the question. Evidence must govern the claim.

12 · Living Technology

18 · Research Integrity

A witness-building system must be able to discover that it was wrong

The long-term strength of the Codex depends on methodological honesty. The system may explore relationships among consciousness, environment, rhythm, language, materials, astronomy, ecology, intention, creativity, invention, and outcomes. It must do so without presuming the relationship is already proven.

Required research disciplines

  • Pre-record hypotheses, intended interventions, expected observations, and stop conditions when practical.
  • Use comparison windows, baselines, blinded or randomized conditions where the question supports them.
  • Preserve null results, failures, contradictory witnesses, and abandoned models.
  • Track multiple comparisons and avoid selecting only meaningful-looking coincidences after the fact.
  • Separate subjective outcomes from instrument measurements while preserving both.
  • Publish methods, schema versions, exclusions, uncertainty, and limitations alongside conclusions.
  • Use outside expertise for domains requiring specialized scientific, medical, engineering, historical, or legal knowledge.
The purpose of the witness archive is not to make every pattern true. It is to make patterns visible enough to be questioned well.
